consolidated-books.db: how it was built and what it means
=============================================================

No majority-rule conflicts: every book/column where the three databases
disagreed had a clear 2-of-3 majority.

Method: for every book (matched by goodreads_book_id) and every column in
`books` (run timestamps excluded, since source_file/first_loaded_at/
last_updated_at never agree across three separate real ingestion runs by
design, and aren't book data anyway), and for every book's full set of
warnings, the value at least 2 of the 3 source databases (Kristina's
books.db, partner-anthony/books.db, partner-abigail/books.db) hold exactly
is used.

Why there are 0 conflicts here specifically: every difference found in
comparison.md traces back to a gap in rules.md. It never says whether to
normalize ISBN check-digit case, what to call a zero-pad that still fails
its checksum, which field/wording a blank-ISBN warning uses, or whether
missing text is NULL vs ''. None of that is a disagreement about what
rules.md actually states. Gaps like that are resolvable by majority vote;
a real disagreement about the rules themselves would not be, and would
show up here as a conflict instead of being silently decided.

One more thing worth knowing: Anthony's and Abigail's databases already
agree with each other on every row (Abigail aligned her own code to match
his before this comparison was run). So in practice, every majority vote
here resolves to their shared value. consolidated-books.db ends up
identical to Anthony's and Abigail's databases on every field that ever
differed from Kristina's. Majority rule didn't invent a new answer; it
just formalizes "where two of the three of us already agree, go with
that" as a reproducible script instead of a one-off hand edit.

See comparison.md's "Why our data differs" section for the full
category-by-category breakdown this was built from.
